Told What To Do

A five-joint robot arm, a bench of coloured balls, and a sentence. “Pick up the green balls and put them in their respective bucket.” The sentence is turned into twelve numbers by Qwen3-Embedding and handed to the arm alongside the positions and colours of everything on the bench. Nothing about the motion is scripted at run time: the arm learned it by imitating a controller that was scripted, and which never sees the sentence.

What the arm actually reads

Ball and bucket positions and colours, straight from the simulator — not from the camera. Plus its own joint angles, what it is holding, what it has already binned, and twelve numbers that are all it ever sees of the sentence.

The Arm eyes panel is a rendering of what a wrist camera would see. Nothing reads those pixels. Making them the arm's only input is a separate problem, and a hard one: measured over 200 episodes, a wrist camera can see all the candidate balls at only 28% of the moments the arm has to choose between them.

Who is driving

The baked brain. It reads the sentence; the scripted controller does not, and is shown only so you can see what the arm is capable of when the language is taken out of the problem.
