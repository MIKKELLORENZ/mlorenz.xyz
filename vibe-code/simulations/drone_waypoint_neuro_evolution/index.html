Learning to Fly

A 1 kg quadrotor sitting on a pad with four rotors and no idea what they do. It has to take off, work out which way is up, and fly to waypoints scattered around a room — through pillars, doorways and shelving. Touch anything and the flight is over. Nothing is scripted, there is no attitude controller underneath, and there is no gradient descent: brains are mutated, scored, and bred.

Curriculum

The ratchet promotes when the mean drone reaches waypoints reliably for three generations running, and never demotes.

Population

One episode ranks the waypoint chain as much as the brain. More episodes, less luck. 96 × 4 is what produced the built-in champion — turn them down to watch, not to train.

The reigning champion keeps an untouched seat this many generations after being beaten.

Mutation

Both gates narrow the search as the run matures: first no fresh random brains, then no gene mixing at all — pure hill climbing.

These are training scores, on the missions the population was selected against — the best line is partly luck. The champion figure in the header is different: after every generation the top brain re-flies six fixed courses it was never trained on, and only that decides who is champion. Without it, one lucky generation holds the title forever.
