Holding the Plasma

A ring of ionised gas at two hundred thousand amps, held in mid-air by magnets, and violently unstable — squash it into an oval and it falls out of the machine in about six milliseconds. Nineteen coils fight it. The controller sees only what a real machine sees: seventy-two noisy magnetic probes, one step stale. No gradients here either — brains are mutated, scored, and bred.

Task

Interface

The full contract is the published TCV one: 34 flux loops, 38 field probes, 19 coil currents, the ohmic difference, Ip, the previous action, the target boundary and the discharge phase — 149 inputs, 19 voltages — plus 70 temporal neurons: the magnetic observer's estimate, multi-lag differences from 0.1 to 6.4 ms, leaky integrals, and the probe array differenced against 0.8 ms ago. The published vector is memoryless, and a controller that cannot tell a plasma sitting high from one falling fast cannot stabilise either. The trimmed one keeps eight probes and five coils: enough to learn vertical stabilisation, nowhere near enough to control a shape.

Curriculum

Stage 0 is a circular plasma — nearly neutral, and the only rung on which a random brain survives long enough to be told apart from another random brain. Stage 1 adds elongation and with it the instability. Stage 2 opens the whole registry. The ratchet promotes on the survival of the better half of the population in the newest task — the roster is half deliberate wreckage, so a whole-population mean never gets there — and it never demotes.

Population

Real training happens in train.js on as many cores as you have. In here, turn the population down to watch and up to believe.

Mutation

Sigma is a multiple of each layer's own initial spread, not an absolute step. It has to be: the output layer starts near zero so a fresh controller commands nothing, and an absolute mutation large enough to matter in the input layer saturates a 1.4 kV supply and disrupts the plasma in one millisecond.

Coil currents

How much current is in each magnet right now. F1-F8 are the outer magnets and E1-E8 the inner ones, which together set the shape. OH1 and OH2 drive the current in the gas. G is the small fast one inside the vessel that catches the plasma when it starts to fall.

Every magnet changes every sensor reading, so there is no single knob for any one job. That is the part that is hard.
