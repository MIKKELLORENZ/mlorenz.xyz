Eight rotors

Corner (sR,sF,sA) of a cube in the 3-space orthogonal to the thrust axis. Seven of the eight Walsh patterns are the controls; the eighth does nothing at all.

Six rotation planes

Three tilt planes contain the thrust axis — rotate in one and the drone falls over. Three heading planes do not, and are the exact 4D generalisation of yaw.

What makes this actually 4D ▾

Orientation is a 4×4 matrix in SO(4), not a quaternion — unit quaternions double-cover SO(3), which is a different group.

Angular velocity is a bivector with C(4,2) = 6 components. There is no axis of rotation; that is a 3D coincidence, true only because the orthogonal complement of a plane happens to be a line when n = 3.

Inertia is four numbers, not six. The six plane moments come from the mass quadrupole as Iab = Ja + Jb, so they cannot be chosen freely — picking six would describe no physical body.

Eight rotors at the corners of a cube. The map from rotor thrusts to (thrust, 3 tilt torques, 3 heading torques) is seven Walsh functions on {−1,+1}³, which are mutually orthogonal — so all seven are independently commandable with condition number 1. An octahedron of six rotors would slave each tilt torque to a heading torque: you could not lean without spinning.

Torque-free motion conserves both Casimirs of so(4) — ‖L‖² and the Pfaffian, the second of which has no 3D analogue. The bundled test suite checks all of this numerically.
