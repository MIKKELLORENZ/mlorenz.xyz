# What actually worked

A post-mortem of the run that produced the brain in `champion.js` — the one the
browser viewer flies. The README explains *what the project is*; this file
explains *which decisions the shipped elite is actually made of*, in rough order
of how much they mattered, with the measurement behind each one.

---

## The brain that shipped

| | |
|---|---|
| file | `champion.js` (generated by `bake.js`, loaded by `index.html`) |
| architecture | `216 → 48 → 32 → 8`, tanh hidden, sigmoid out, 12,248 parameters |
| generation | 1604, population 110, 8 episodes/generation |
| weights | sha1 `10cc674d3599` |
| picked from | `pop#5` of the final population — **not** the brain the log called champion |
| source | `training/ckpt_final.json` → `pick_champion.js` → `ckpt_final_picked.json` |
| held-out score | 8.13 waypoints/episode across 4 unseen seed banks (worst bank 6.75 at 8 eps/bank; 5.50 at the 6 eps/bank the pick itself used) |
| reference | hand-written PD autopilot, same seeds: 18.31 wp/ep — the evolved brain is **44%** of it |
| training cost | 1,604 generations, ~250 min wall clock on 6 workers |

Nothing about hover, level, up or attitude is scripted anywhere. The net
commands eight rotor throttles directly, at 30 Hz, over 120 Hz physics.

### The shape of the run

| | gen 435 (fixed bank) | gen 511 | gen 1108 | gen 1604 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| waypoints/ep, held out | 0.00 | 0.00 | 6.29 | **8.13** |
| mean airtime | 4.0 s | 5.2 s | 60.3 s | 56.9 s |
| crash rate | 100% | 100% | 31% | 39% |

The whole first third of the project is flat. Everything below is a list of the
things that had to be true simultaneously before the curve moved at all.

---

## 1. The mission bank must rotate every generation

**The single largest effect in the project.** A bank fixed for the run is the
obvious choice — it makes two generations comparable and stops the champion from
being whoever drew the easiest chain — and it bought a **435-generation plateau
at exactly 0.00 held-out waypoints** while the training log climbed respectably.

Drones died around 8 s in, so they almost never reached waypoint 2. Six fixed
missions is therefore a training signal of six *first legs*, and six directions
are memorisable. The tell was an asymmetry no navigation policy would produce:
at gen 435, **61% of all deaths were against the −w wall specifically**, while
random brains as a control crashed symmetrically across all six lateral walls
(~57 each). The population had evolved open-loop "fly roughly that way" and was
not reading the waypoint sensor at all — and since it was scored on exactly
those six missions, that was locally optimal and unescapable.

Resuming the same population against fresh missions priced the damage:

| | training bank | fresh missions |
|---|---|---|
| best fitness | 1238 | 475 |
| waypoints/ep | 0.50 | **0.00** |

Flying ability was real and transferred; navigation did not exist.
`train.js`'s `bankFor()` now marches the bank forward every generation. Two
consequences that had to be handled rather than tolerated:

- the log's `best` column stops being comparable across generations, so
  `championFit` is cleared each generation and **nothing is picked from the log**;
- training seeds start at `TRAIN_SEED_BASE = 2000000`, because the naive formula
  walks into the held-out banks (60k–93k) within ~50 generations and silently
  contaminates the only independent measurement in the project.

## 2. Mutation proportional to each layer's own scale

Without this the network **cannot be evolved at all** — it is not a tuning
refinement. The output layer is initialised at 0.03× the He scale, so its
natural weight size is ≈ 0.0075 while the first hidden layer's is ≈ 0.096. A
flat sigma of 0.09 is a sensible step for the hidden layers and *thirteen times
the entire width of the output layer*: one mutation event saturates a rotor and
the airframe flips.

Measured on the fixed benchmark bank: generation 1 had **99% of the population
leaving the ground; generation 2, bred from gen 1's elites on identical
missions, had 12%.** It read exactly like a crash-penalty or reward-shaping
problem and was neither — the children were being lobotomised in the breeding
loop. `Net.mutate()` now scales sigma by `initScales[l] / 0.1`, and the rare 3%
full-reset branch gets the same treatment. Head to head over 25 generations:
100% takeoff and a climbing champion against 78% and a frozen one
(`training/log_aba|abb|abc.txt`, `log_mhi|mlo.txt`).

## 3. Output init 0.03 — chosen on fitness *spread*, not on the theory

The structural argument said 4D wants a smaller output init than 3D: tilt torque
from a random output layer grows as √(rotor count), so eight corners is √2 more
tumble than four arms, into three tilt planes instead of two. `probe.js` swept it
(40 brains × 3 episodes) and the argument turned out to have nothing to fix —
arming is saturated everywhere below 0.05:

| outInit | left ground | armed | mean air | mean wp | **fitness spread** |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.050 | 100% | 81% | 2.09 s | 0.000 | 82 |
| **0.030** | **100%** | **99%** | **3.53 s** | **0.008** | **182** |
| 0.020 | 100% | 100% | 4.37 s | 0.000 | 98 |
| 0.006 | 100% | 100% | 6.18 s | 0.000 | 107 |

What separates the rows is the **spread**, which is the selection signal, and it
peaks at 0.03 — also the only setting where a random brain ever touches a
waypoint. Below 0.02 the fleet climbs placidly into the ceiling: tidy, uniform,
and unrankable. A population that all scores the same is a generation the GA
cannot use.

## 4. Make the box observable: sensor range 32 m

Inherited from the 3D project at 14 m, and from the spawn point **fourteen of
the twenty-four rangefinders read exactly zero** — the walls were 15 m away and
simply out of range, so 28 of the 216 net inputs were dead and the drone was
blind to the wall it was drifting into until about a second before impact. Range
is now 32 m, longer than the room's 30 m longest span, so the eight axis rays
always return a real distance in all four dimensions. Less "making it easier"
than "making it observable".

## 5. Damping, not altitude: the fix for a 100% crash rate

The run sat at 100% crash and 4.0 s airtime for a hundred generations. The
champion's own flight log showed it drifting into a side wall — a navigation
story. Tallying deaths across **60 brains × 3 episodes** (`forensic.js`) told a
different one: the commonest single cause was the **floor at 28%**, and the
median death came at 4.9 s with the airframe at **55° from level**. That is
attitude control, not navigation, and it is the bill for three tilt planes
instead of two.

Two candidate fixes, run from an identical seed:

| configuration | best | avg | airtime | took off |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline (`log_fX`) | 826 | 330 | 3.8 s | 100% |
| arming height 1.2 → 2.5 m (`log_fY`) | 556 | 222 | 2.5 s | 80% |
| **arming 2.5 m + damping ×2.5 (`log_fZ`)** | **918** | **395** | **4.4 s** | **100%** |

The middle row is the informative one: raising the arming height *alone* is a
clear loss, because arming is also what starts airtime accruing, so it moves the
first rung of the ladder out of reach. The damping (`CANG_LIN 0.08`,
`CANG_QUAD 0.05`) more than pays for that, which is how we know it is the
damping doing the work. Raising `TAKEOFF_H` also forced
`WP_FLOOR − WP_RADIUS > TAKEOFF_H` (4.5 − 1.5 = 3.0 > 2.5), or a drone could
hover just under the arming threshold — where the floor is still a landing pad
and cannot kill it — and clip low waypoints it never had to fly to.

## 6. Pricing survival comparably to approach

The 3D reward economy (10 points/s of airtime, capped at 200) left the 4D
population stuck: measured over 100 generations, mean airtime sat flat at 4.0 s
with a 100% crash rate, because staying alive was worth 40 points against an
average score of 250 while drifting hopefully toward the waypoint paid up to
600. With three tilt planes to keep still, there was no reason to bother. Now
`AIR_W = 30, AIR_CAP = 540` against `PROGRESS_W = 600` and `WP_REWARD = 1000` —
compared head to head as `log_airold` (766 best / 235 avg) vs `log_airnew`
(903 / 331).

The rest of the ledger that survived: a deliberately small `CRASH_PEN = 100`
(the forfeited future is the real cost), a speed bonus that decays with leg
time, and an efficiency charge only on distance flown beyond 1.35× what the leg
actually needed.

## 7. Time as the resource, not a hover penalty

Each drone is born with an 18 s clock and gains 10 s per waypoint
(`world4d.js`). A drone that hovers prettily and finds nothing runs its clock
down and freezes with its score locked; one that keeps arriving keeps flying.
That removes the stable-hover local optimum without penalising hovering
directly — a penalty term would have to be balanced against the crash penalty,
and this does not.

## 8. Fitness must be a property of the brain alone

One shared sensor-noise RNG advanced once per *living* drone per tick, so as
neighbours started dying at different times an elite re-flying the identical
fixed bank scored differently from one generation to the next. Fitness stopped
being a property of the brain and the GA was ranking noise. Every drone now
draws from its own stream, seeded identically — common random numbers: the whole
fleet is perturbed the same way and the only difference left is the brain.

The same class of bug, one level down: Box-Muller's spare normal is cached **on
the RNG object**, never in a module-level variable. With one shared cache a
leftover spare leaks between consumers, and a worker computes a different
episode than the main thread for the same `(missionSeed, noiseSeed)`.

And one level down again: physics overrides ride along in the episode options
(`EP_OPTS.phys`), because each worker thread loads its own copy of `drone4d.js`
and a constant patched on the main thread is invisible to it — the fleet would
be silently scored under different physics than the log claims.

## 9. Champion grace

A brain lives or dies on a handful of episodes, and one unlucky waypoint chain
can wipe out a genuinely good one. The reigning champion keeps an untouched seat
for 6 generations after being beaten; defending the title refreshes it. It costs
one population slot. The grace events run all through `log_final.txt` — the
title genuinely changes hands, and is genuinely sheltered, dozens of times.

## 10. Pick the shipped brain on evidence it was not selected by

With a rotating bank, whichever brain tops the final generation is partly there
because it drew that generation's missions. `pick_champion.js` takes the top 12
of the final population plus the logged champion, folds elitism duplicates by
weight hash, and flies every survivor over **four independent held-out banks**,
with the criterion fixed in the source before any results are seen: mean
waypoints across banks, tie-broken by the worst bank, because bank-to-bank
spread is the luck detector.

It put `pop#5` (**8.13**) above the logged champion (7.88). That re-ranking is
the entire reason the step exists, and `pop#5` is what the browser flies.

## The reward function, term by term

§5, §6 and §7 each describe one change to the ledger. This is the complete
ledger, from [drone4d.js](drone4d.js#L164-L181) and
[world4d.js](world4d.js#L143-L156), with the physical rule each term is priced
against.

### The ledger

| term | value | the mechanic it is tied to |
|---|---|---|
| **waypoint** | **+1,000** | banked when the 4-position is within **1.5 m** of the target — a 4-ball, whose volume goes as *r*⁴, so the 3D project's 1.2 m would have been far harder here; the drone has to null a fourth error term to enter it |
| leg progress, banked | +600 | the leg's whole progress term, paid at arrival |
| speed bonus | `max(0, 300 − 15 × legTime)` | zero for any leg over **20 s** — twice the 10 s a waypoint puts back on the clock |
| efficiency charge | `−min(300, 5 × (legDist − 1.35 × legInitDist))` | 4D path length actually flown beyond 1.35× the straight line; **banked only on completed legs** |
| clock | **+10 s** | on the drone's own 18 s birth clock |
| leg progress, live | `600 × (1 − bestDist / legInitDist)` | **closest approach ever achieved** on the leg, never the current distance |
| controlled airtime | `min(540, 30 × airTime)` | accrues only while **armed** *and* within ~72° of level (`cos tilt > 0.3`) |
| takeoff | +120 | once, when `armed` latches at **2.5 m** altitude |
| crash | −100 | instant elimination on any wall, or the floor once armed; no HP, no bump |

### Why each term is shaped by the physics

- **Arming is a physical state, not a score threshold.** Until the drone has
  once cleared `TAKEOFF_H = 2.5 m`, the floor is a pad: it supports the airframe,
  bleeds sideways velocity through the skids and damps rotation
  ([drone4d.js](drone4d.js#L553-L570)). After that it is lethal like every other
  surface, and airtime starts counting. The 120-point takeoff bonus is the first
  rung of the ladder; §5 shows what happens when the rung is raised without paying
  for it, and why `WP_FLOOR − WP_RADIUS > TAKEOFF_H` has to hold.
- **Airtime is gated on attitude.** Without the `UPRIGHT_MIN` gate a tumbling
  drone that happens to stay inside the room farms the same airtime as one that is
  flying, and the population learns to spin instead of hover. The gate is the
  reason "airtime" in this document means *controlled* flight.
- **The airtime cap is exactly one birth clock.** 540 / 30 = 18 s. A drone that
  arms and hovers perfectly for its whole starting clock earns 540 + 120 = 660 and
  then freezes; one arrival is 1,600 plus a speed bonus plus ten more seconds of
  clock. The hover ceiling is reachable without ever reading the waypoint sensor;
  the arrival is not, and hovering never extends the clock (§7). That is the whole
  ordering — *waypoints ≫ closest approach ≫ controlled airtime* — and §6 is the
  measurement of what happened when airtime was priced too low to be worth
  learning at all.
- **Progress is a ratchet on closest approach.** Orbiting the target, overshooting
  it, or drifting back out earns nothing; only a new best distance pays. Combined
  with the 4-ball radius this is what makes the last metre of a leg the expensive
  one.
- **The efficiency charge is deliberately not live.** It is banked in `_arrive`
  against legs actually completed. Charging the leg in progress would tax exactly
  the exploratory flailing early generations need in order to find flight — the
  opposite choice from the boats sim, where the same charge runs every tick,
  because a boat that cannot fly still sails.
- **The crash penalty is small on purpose.** A crash ends the episode, so the real
  cost is every waypoint the drone would have gone on to reach. Pricing it at 100
  instead of, say, 1,000 keeps the first generations — which crash 100% of the
  time — rankable on *how far they got before crashing* rather than all sharing
  one large negative number.

### The rule the reward cannot enforce

None of this tells the network that eight corner rotors form an orthogonal
Walsh mixer, or that three tilt planes need damping before a waypoint is
reachable. The ledger only ranks; the section below is what made a ranking
climbable.

## Environment facts that made any of this learnable

These are not GA tuning; they are the reasons a search over 12,248 weights could
succeed at all.

- **Eight rotors at the corners of a cube.** The map from the eight thrusts to
  (total thrust, 3 tilt torques, 3 heading torques) is seven of the eight Walsh
  functions on {−1,+1}³ — mutually orthogonal, rank 7, condition number exactly
  1. No control direction is weak. Six rotors on the ±axes (an octahedron) lose
  rank: each tilt torque becomes slaved to a heading torque, so the drone cannot
  lean without spinning. The network is never told any of this and has to
  discover it, but it is *there to be found*.
- **Inertia derived from a real mass distribution.** Six plane moments generated
  by four numbers, `I₍ₐ,ᵦ₎ = Jₐ + Jᵦ`, chosen to reproduce the 3D reference
  quadrotor exactly (0.018 tilt / 0.030 heading). The attitude dynamics run on
  timescales already known to be flyable, and the gearing (`TMAX`, `ARM`)
  matches the same climb margin and torque-per-newton.
- **A correct integrator.** RK4 on the momentum step, and a *trapezoidal*
  orientation update — advancing orientation on end-of-step angular velocity is
  first order no matter how good either half is. Asserting the convergence
  *order* rather than a fixed tolerance is what caught it; the error fell 850×.
- **A hand-written PD pilot, written first.** When a GA fails, "the task is
  hard", "the reward is broken" and "the task is impossible" all look identical.
  `autopilot.js` flies 18–19 waypoints per episode, which proves the airframe is
  controllable, the mixer is right and the arrival test is satisfiable — so
  every remaining problem is a search problem. It is also the number the evolved
  result is quoted against, and it can take over mid-flight in the viewer.
- **A temporal input window instead of recurrence.** 63 channels at lags
  0,1,2,3,4,6,8,10, with per-channel depth: gyro 6 deep (the D term of an
  attitude loop), gravity 8 (slow attitude drift), rangefinders only 2 (range
  and closing rate). 216 inputs, and no hidden state to evolve.
- **Two compass channels.** Gravity pins the thrust axis but says nothing about
  rotation within the orthogonal 3-space — three whole degrees of freedom, the
  4D generalisation of yaw. World +x and world +w in body coordinates close the
  gap; the fourth direction is then determined. Spawn attitude is randomised *in
  the heading planes only*, so a fixed world-frame heading cannot be baked into
  the weights.

## What did not work, and cost time

| tried | result |
|---|---|
| fixed 6-mission bank for the whole run | 435 generations at 0.00 held-out waypoints |
| flat mutation sigma across layers | takeoff 99% → 12% in a single generation |
| raising the arming height alone | best 826 → 556, takeoff 100% → 80% |
| lowering the output init on the √N tumble argument | arming is already saturated below 0.05; the theory had nothing to fix, and the spread got worse |
| trusting the training log's `best` column | would have shipped a brain 0.25 waypoints worse than `pop#5` |
| diagnosing from the champion's flight log alone | said "navigation"; the population-wide tally said "attitude — floor, 28%" |

## Reproducing it

```
node test_math.js                              # 65 assertions — run this first
node train.js --pop 110 --episodes 8 --workers 6 --tag final
node pick_champion.js training/ckpt_final.json 12
node bake.js training/ckpt_final_picked.json   # → champion.js
node eval_brain.js training/ckpt_final_picked.json
```

Defaults that produced the shipped brain: `mrate 0.12`, `msigma 0.09`,
`grace 6`, `turb 0.15`, rotation on, immigrant gate 3000, crossover gate 14000,
episode hard cap 120 s, `maxArrivals 30`, `minSep 8`. The airframe numbers
(`CANG 0.08/0.05`, `TAKEOFF_H 2.5`, `SENSOR_RANGE 32`, `WP_RADIUS 1.5`,
`WP_FLOOR 4.5`) are defaults in `drone4d.js` / `room4d.js` and need no flags.

The browser loads `champion.js` alongside the *same* physics files the trainer
used, so `index.html` is flying the episode, not replaying a recording.
