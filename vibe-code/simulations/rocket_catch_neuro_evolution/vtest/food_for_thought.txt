Evolution runs but never produces landing behavior. For each question below, answer with
concrete evidence (numbers, plots, code references) — not "yes, that's handled."

1. SOLVABILITY: Can a hand-written controller (PID/LQR, or even a scripted suicide burn)
   land in this exact sim, with these exact actuator limits and observations? If not, the
   GA is innocent — the environment is the bug. Report TWR at near-dry mass and minimum
   throttle: if min_throttle × TWR > 1, hovering is impossible and only a timed hoverslam
   works. Confirm control authority is sufficient at every phase.

2. FITNESS GRADIENT: Plot the fitness distribution of generation 0. If all random genomes
   score roughly the same (e.g., everything crashes and gets a flat penalty), selection has
   zero signal. Does fitness improve smoothly as behavior gets marginally better — closer to
   pad, slower at impact, more upright? List every fitness term, its weight, and its typical
   magnitude at gen 0 vs. a hypothetical near-landing. Then hunt for degenerate optima: can a
   genome score well by hovering until timeout, drifting away slowly, or dying early?

3. CURRICULUM: Are genomes evaluated on the full problem from day one? Show results of
   starting trivially easy (5–20 m up, upright, near-zero velocity, above the pad) and
   widening the initial-condition envelope only as success rate rises. The flip maneuver
   especially should not be expected to emerge cold from random weights.

4. OBSERVATIONS: List every NN input, its frame, and its normalization. Is position
   expressed relative to the pad? Are all inputs scaled to roughly [-1, 1]? How is attitude
   encoded — raw Euler angles will pass through gimbal-lock territory (~90° pitch) during
   the belly flop; use quaternion components or rotation-matrix rows instead. Are angular
   rates, vertical velocity, and fuel fraction included?

5. ACTIONS: How do raw NN outputs map to actuators? Confirm: throttle clamped to
   [min_throttle, 1] with realistic relight/ignition constraints, gimbal with both
   deflection AND rate limits, flaps/RCS for the orbiter, and first-order actuator lag.
   Are outputs squashed (tanh) then mapped, or raw and saturating?

6. EVALUATION NOISE: Are initial conditions randomized per rollout? If yes, how many
   rollouts per genome, and do all genomes in a generation share the same seed set (common
   random numbers)? One noisy rollout per genome means selection amplifies luck, not skill.
   If instead everything is one fixed scenario, flag the overfitting risk.

7. GA MECHANICS: Report population size, genome length (weight count), mutation operator
   and sigma, crossover scheme, elitism count, and any diversity mechanism (speciation,
   novelty, fitness sharing). Is crossover on direct-encoded weights helping or scrambling
   (competing-conventions problem)? Plot best/mean fitness and population diversity over
   generations — is this premature convergence or a flat landscape?

8. NETWORK SIZE: How many parameters are being evolved? Direct-encoded GAs degrade badly
   past a few thousand weights. Is the net feedforward (fine if state is fully observable)
   or recurrent (harder to evolve — justify it)? Does the output activation range match the
   action mapping?

9. TIME AND TERMINATION: What is the control frequency vs. physics dt? What exactly counts
   as "landed" (contact speed, lateral speed, tilt angle, sustained for N steps) and as
   "crashed"? Does the timeout cut off genomes mid-descent in ways that punish slower but
   correct approaches? Verify the contact/leg model doesn't make touchdown physically chaotic.

10. ORBITER PHASING: Is one network expected to handle aero belly descent, the flip, and the
    terminal burn in a single policy? Compare against: separate controllers per phase with an
    explicit handoff, a phase flag as input, or evolving the booster case first and
    transferring. Also verify the aero model itself: do the flaps actually provide enough
    pitch authority, and is center-of-pressure vs. CG placement stable in belly attitude?

Finish with: (a) the top 3 suspected root causes ranked by evidence, (b) the single cheapest
experiment to confirm each, and (c) trajectory plots of the current best genome per phase.
