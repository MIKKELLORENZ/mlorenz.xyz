Learning to be Caught

A 250 tonne steel cylinder falls out of the sky with no landing legs, and its only acceptable ending is to be plucked out of the air by two arms on a tower, 120 metres up, at walking pace, inside a metre and a half. Nothing here is scripted and there is no gradient descent: the controller was bred.

The network flies both stages — the booster, which falls tail-first on three grid fins, and the Ship, which falls flat on its back at 70 m/s and has to rotate ninety degrees in the last kilometre. One genome, told which vehicle it is in, and told each flight whether the tower is cleared or whether it has to go somewhere else.

Training happens on a 128-core machine, not in this tab. This page only replays what came out.
