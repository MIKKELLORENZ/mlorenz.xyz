Quick guide

  1. Drop in an image, or use the demo. Choose Cutout for a flat extrusion, or Variable relief for a brightness-based height map.
  2. Use + Region to draw a rectangle, or choose Polygon in the region editor. Click to place points; double-click to close. Each region has its own conversion settings.
  3. With Select, click a region to edit it. Drag inside to move it, drag the square handles to resize it, and drag a point to reshape it. Drag the small circle in the middle of an edge to add a point. Double-click a point, or click it and press Delete, to remove it. Convert to polygon turns a rectangle into four editable points.
  4. Rename a layer by double-clicking its name in the layer list or on its label in the region editor, or edit the Name field above its settings.
  5. Adjust the white cutoff, dimensions, thickness, and smoothing. Inspect the model, then export STL. Save project keeps the image, layers and settings in one file you can open again.
Region layersRectangles and polygons use original-image coordinates. Each has independent mode, cutoff, thickness, smoothing, gamma, and optional local inversion. Resizing, moving, and reshaping preserve those settings. Top regions replace the settings below them, including white holes; they do not stack extra copies of the mesh. Disable the Whole image layer to keep only selected regions. Forward and Back change priority; Duplicate copies a region with its settings. Up to twelve regions are supported.
Polygon drawingUse three to 64 points. Click the first point, double-click, press Enter, or use Close polygon to finish. Undo point (or Backspace) removes the last point; Escape cancels an unfinished drawing. Concave polygons are supported. Crossing edges are rejected, so use a simple closed outline.
KeyboardCtrl+Z undoes and Ctrl+Shift+Z (or Ctrl+Y) redoes any layer, region or setting change. In the region editor, arrow keys nudge the selected region or point by one pixel (Shift: ten), Delete removes the selected point or region, and Escape clears the point selection.
Base plateAdds a solid plate of the chosen thickness under the whole image rectangle and stands the model on it, so separate dark areas print as one piece. White areas then become plate instead of holes. The result is still one closed solid.
Region boundariesThe combined result is a single height field. Settings transition across one mesh cell at region boundaries, giving a short ramp between different thicknesses. Increase Mesh detail for a sharper transition. Very small regions may need higher detail.
Export layersAll enabled layers exports the combined preview. Selected layer only exports that entire layer in isolation at the same image position and scale, without overrides from other layers. Hidden layers are not exported.
Fit & appearanceFit measures the actual mesh after the current rotation and fills the available view with a 5% margin on each limiting side. Empty image borders are ignored. Changing conversion settings preserves your camera view; a new image fits automatically. Color controls affect only the preview; Reset colors restores the original appearance.
Invert photo colorsUse the toggle below the image upload to swap light and dark in either mode. The processed preview and STL update together. Transparent areas remain empty.
White cutoffPixels with processed brightness below the cutoff become material. Pixels at or above it are removed. At 128, darker half-tones remain; at 255, only pure white is excluded. Relief starts at 250 to keep most tones.
CutoutRemaining areas within this layer have one constant thickness, with a flat bottom.
Variable reliefDarker areas are thicker. Black reaches the maximum; lighter material approaches the minimum. The white cutoff still creates holes. The bottom remains flat.
SmoothingGaussian blur before thresholding and height mapping. The value is a percentage of image width, so its scale remains consistent when detail changes. More smoothing rounds contours and softens relief; small features can disappear.
Mesh detailSubdivisions along the longest image side; 512 is the default. 1024 and 2048 have four and sixteen times as many triangles, take longer to rebuild after each change, and can produce STL files of hundreds of megabytes. More subdivisions capture smaller features, but going past the image’s own pixel count adds triangles without recovering detail absent from the input.
Image widthWidth of the whole image, including white margins. Height follows the image aspect ratio. The model footprint below the preview reports actual solid bounds. Coordinates are intended as millimeters; choose millimeters when importing into a slicer because STL does not store units.
TransparencyTransparent input is treated as white, including with inversion enabled. Partial transparency is composited onto white before smoothing.

Relief formula

thickness = minimum + (maximum − minimum) × (1 − brightness / 255)gamma

For printing

Without a base plate, each separate dark island becomes a separate closed solid. Turn on Add a base plate to join them, or combine the pieces on a base in your slicer or CAD tool. Verify fine walls and unsupported details against your printer’s capabilities.

The STL has a flat underside, closed sides, and a triangulated top. Hole boundaries follow interpolated brightness contours; image Y is flipped to preserve the image’s appearance when viewed from above. Without a base plate, a white hole is empty all the way through, in relief mode too.

Source & portability

This is a self-contained, editable HTML/JavaScript program. Save a copy and open it offline. There are no CDNs, analytics, uploads, or external packages. Modern desktop Chromium and Firefox browsers are recommended. Very large detail settings need more memory.
